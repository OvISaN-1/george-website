GEORGE'SDreamkeeper

A slow, cosy garden to wind down in. Worries drift in. Your helpers turn them into happy thoughts, and every happy thought brings you stars to spend.

  • 1️⃣Pick a helper at the bottom, then tap a glowing tile to plant it. Start the next wave early for bonus stars. The helpers on the left do the most damage. The three after the dotted line are support: they help the others.
  • 2️⃣Worries drift along the path. Helpers turn them into happy thoughts and stars. Tap a helper to upgrade it.
  • 3️⃣Mix helpers: slow them down, light them up and calm them, then everything hits harder.
  • 💛Every worry becomes a happy thought, and the stars come with it. If one reaches the nest it still turns into a happy thought, but you get fewer stars and the garden is a little less calm.

Make it sleepy

Best before bed, not in it. Sit up, keep the screen dim, and put it away when the timer says goodnight. Screens close to sleep can make it harder to drift off, so this game is made slow and gentle, and it asks you to stop.

If a worry feels big, tell a grown-up you trust. A game can help you feel calmer, but talking helps most. You can also call Childline free on 0800 1111 any time.

      For parents: why it works this way

      Dreamkeeper is a game, not a treatment, and it is not a replacement for talking. These are the ideas behind it.

      • 🧠Worries are named, then answeredEach enemy is a common "thinking trap" kids' CBT resources use (Fortune Teller, Mind Reader, All-or-Nothing, All My Fault and so on, see Choosing Therapy and Child Mind Institute). When one becomes a star, a kinder thought replaces it.
      • 🌬️Slow breathingThe breathing break is box breathing (in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4), a standard calming exercise (see Raising Children Network).
      • 💛Three good thingsEnding the night on something positive is a low-risk routine. The research behind it is mostly with adults, so treat it as a nice habit, not a cure (see Psychology Today). It uses pick-lists only, so nothing personal is typed or saved.
      • 📵Screens and sleepHealth bodies advise screen-free time before bed (see NHS and National Sleep Foundation). So this game is slow, dim and warm, never flashes, has no scores or timers to chase, and ends itself with the sleep timer. It works best earlier in the wind-down, sitting up, not in bed.
      • 🛏️Worry talks belong in the daytimeTalking through bigger worries is best done earlier in the day, not in bed (see Connecticut Children's). If a worry is big or keeps coming back, please talk to your child, and to your GP or school if it's affecting sleep or school. Childline (0800 1111, free, UK) is there for children at any time.
